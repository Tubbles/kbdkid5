# CLAUDE.md

Guidance for Claude Code working in this repository.

## What this is

kbdkid5, the successor of kbdkid4 (`~/dev/kbdkid4`, https://github.com/Tubbles/kbdkid4): a wireless split keyboard whose 3D-printed parts (tray, switch plate, PCB mock, fused mock) are built by FreeCAD scripts in `scripts/`, running headless in a container. [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md) holds the design and every dimension, [doc/components.md](doc/components.md) the part research with sources, [PLAN.md](PLAN.md) the phases, `doc/work/` the work items with their status, `doc/log/` dated decision logs.

There is no PCB design yet (the user does it later in LibrePCB). Until then `scripts/layout.py` is the ground truth for the geometry: keys, outline, mounting drills, the parts in the tray and the joint. It is plain Python with no FreeCAD import, so `scripts/check_layout.py` can check it anywhere. The export scripts read it. When DESIGN.md and `layout.py` disagree, that is a bug in one of them: fix it, don't pick one silently.

## Layout, check, mocks and plate

`scripts/layout.py` holds DESIGN.md's numbers, each commented with its source (DESIGN.md, a kbdkid4 file, doc/components.md): the keys, the Choc footprint's holes (kbdkid4's `KHA-PG1350` package) and hotswap socket polygons (kbdkid4's `HOTSWAP_SOCKET_POLYGONS_MM`), both mapped onto the board as kbdkid4's `Placement.map_point` maps them, the outline with its slant, the mounting drills, the heights, the tray's dimensions (`scripts/tray_shape.py` re-exports them under kbdkid4's names), the diodes' place (kbdkid4's, for the covered plate's pit), the parts' envelopes with the ledge cut-aways, the parts' mounts (each body the tray adds and each void it cuts a `MountBox`, the figures that wait on a caliper in `MountFits`) and the wire rooms behind the parts' leads, the sockets' cuts in the tray (kbdkid4's tab notch, ledge relief and standoff flat rules from its `export_tray.py`, applied by functions here to the sockets named here, for either half), the joint and the pin parking hole. DESIGN.md's figures that follow from those values are not in `layout.py` but in `check_layout.py`, as `DESIGN_*` constants transcribed independently. The right half is the left one mirrored across `RIGHT_HALF_MIRROR_X_MM`, the outline's center line (x 66.199), with `mirror_for_right_half()` for points: every export mirrors across that one line, so right parts line up with each other as the left ones do (the module docstring says why not x = 0 or each part's own center). A switch's footprint cannot be mirrored (its pins are not symmetric), so a right half footprint is the left one at `mirror_for_right_half(key_center)`, a constraint on the PCB design (DESIGN.md, Frame and units). The tray is built in the left half's frame and mirrored, so `socket_polygons_on_half(key, right=True)` gives a right half socket in that frame: the footprint's mirror image at the left key center. The right half's socket cuts are declared apart from the left's (`RIGHT_SOCKET_*`). The `CHOC_PIN_*` constants are the switch's pin blades as measured on kbdkid4's switch model, but for `CHOC_PIN_CALIPERED_BLADE_THICKNESSES_MM`, the user's caliper figures per pin. `pin_slits()` places the fused mock's slits on them, `pin_slits_of_widths()` gives each pin its own width, and `default_slit_width()` and `default_slit_length()` give the fused mock's default sizes for a `pull_in`.

`scripts/check_layout.py` checks the layout and DESIGN.md's derived figures against it: the outline (also against the key cells and DESIGN.md's insets) and slant, keys and holes, drills, heights, the parts against the cavity wall, the ledge, the standoffs, each other and the sockets, the battery, the sockets against the tray as cut (the ledge and joint block with the notches and reliefs, the wall with the tab pockets, the standoffs with their flats, each at its rule's clearance, and the named sockets against DESIGN.md's), the joint (the pin bosses at the slant's ends, the magnets between them, the bosses and their fills 0.5 from the sockets and clear of the standoffs, 1.0 of plastic between any two of its holes and slots) and the parking hole with its boss, the parts' mounts with the nice!nano clamp's tab and screw head (inside the tray, clear of the parts, the sockets, the standoffs and the joint, the floor's voids short of its underside, the clamp's figures, its tab's strain and its room under the PCB, the openings around the lever and the USB shell, the nice!nano's pry recess in plan against the other parts, the mount bodies, the standoffs and the joint, under the board only within its groove, and the slopes at which a straight tool tip, coming in from the east over the tray as printed, reaches as far under the board as the recess does, and how far a flat tip lifts the board's south end over the clamp's stop) and the wire rooms (clear of all of it), and the switch's blades against the package's pin holes (two independent sources) and inside their default slits. Bodies are checked against each other in plan wherever their z ranges overlap, on sharp-cornered polygons. Every check that involves the sockets runs for both halves, the right one in the left half's frame, right after the left one. It stops at the first failing check and lists every offender in it. A failure is a design conflict: fix DESIGN.md and `layout.py` together, never the check. Run all checks without stopping by importing it and calling each of `CHECKS`.

`scripts/pcb_shape.py` builds the PCB mock from the layout: the sharp outline moved in by `pull_in` and rounded concentric with the layout's corner radius, every hole grown by `pull_in`, any slits at their modelled size, 1.6 thick, flat on z = 0. `build_mock()` checks for one valid solid, the volume against an area computed from the layout alone (no FreeCAD offsets or fillets), the opening count and the outline's rounded corner count. The module has no entry point. `half_outline()`, `half_key_centers()`, `half_drills()` and `all_holes(right)` give either half: for the right one the outline and the drills mirrored, each key's footprint unmirrored at the mirrored key center. It also holds the fused mock's fusing of the PCB layer with the plate (one valid solid of both volumes) and measuring of its faces (each plate cutout on its key's center hole, each slit on its pin at its size), the slit measure shared with the slit test strip. `scripts/export_pcb.py` exports the bare mock for either half as one closed mesh.

`scripts/export_mock.py` exports the fused mock: the PCB mock with slits in place of each key's two pin holes (`slit_width`, `slit_length`) at z 8.0 to 9.6, fused with `build_plate(pattern_shift=0)`, checked as above and written PCB side down (the PCB layer's underside on z = 0). For `right` the outline, plate and drills are mirrored, the footprints not. `scripts/export_slit_test.py` exports a strip of six single-key cells (a 19.05 square of the PCB layer alone, no plate) whose slits are each pin's calipered blade thickness plus the cell's clearance, -0.15 to +0.10 in 0.05 steps, cell k with k notches in its south edge, checked for one valid solid of the PCB layer's volume less the notches, every slit on its pin at its own size and at DESIGN.md's width (its own `DESIGN_*` constants), the notches' places and a closed mesh. The word `wide` makes the wide strip with the same checks: both slits of a cell one width as modelled, 0.5 to 1.0 in 0.1 steps (`DESIGN_WIDE_SLIT_WIDTHS_MM`).

`scripts/socket_test_shape.py` builds the hand-wired frame's three socket retention test strips (work item 008; DESIGN.md "Hand-wired frame test" has the figures), six cells at the key pitch in a strip frame of their own (no layout keys), each with the left half's footprint: the frame strips (`build_frame()`, `pocket` or `lip`) are a plate of the PCB's thickness with the footprint holes grown by `pull_in` (the pocket strip's two pin holes `POCKET_STRIP_PIN_HOLE_DIAMETER_MM`, 3.5 as modelled, instead), pocket walls around the socket's plastic body (`pocket_pieces()`: the lobes cut back to `layout.HOTSWAP_SOCKET_BODY_ENDS_MM` plus `layout.HOTSWAP_SOCKET_FILLS_MM`, `layout.HOTSWAP_SOCKET_BODY_HEIGHT_MM` deep), slotted at the tabs and cut back beside the switch's post and pegs, the lip strip's lips on fingers (`FINGERS`, blades freed by slots through the plate and walls, held at the root by the plate and a block under it, slots and blades chamfered on the bed like the snap hook the tray had until work item 009), and bosses bored for heat-set inserts. The backing (`build_backing()`) is a plate with screw holes and a pad under each lobe. The steps, the print model behind each "as printed" figure, the tabs' slots and the bosses' places are in its docstring and constants. Each builder checks one valid solid against an analytic volume computed from the plan geometry alone (convex polygon clipping with inclusion and exclusion for the walls around the body's overlapping pieces and their cuts, an exact circle and rectangle overlap where a slot meets a footprint hole, integrated by Simpson's rule over a bed chamfer's height). The module has no entry point. `scripts/export_socket_test.py` exports one strip (`pocket`, `lip` or `backing`, plus `pull_in`, `insert_length` and `lip_fit`) and measures it against its own `DESIGN_*` constants: the pockets from their wall faces, the walls' depth, the lips, the fingers from their faces with their strain by the hook's formula (failing above 2 %), their slots, roots and bed chamfers by probing, the tab slots, the pin holes, the bores, the clearances (`distToShape` on the unfused bodies), the notches and the marks (one on the lip strip, two on the pocket strip). For `backing` it also builds both frame strips and checks the stack: no overlap, the contact exactly the bosses' ends and the lip strip's walls', fingers' and blocks' ends, the pads' distance to each frame, the bores over the screw holes, the wire rooms, and the battery clearance in the real tray. Frame strips are written plate top down, the backing bottom down.

`scripts/plate_shape.py` builds the switch plate with kbdkid4's plate pipeline on the layout: the kbdkid3 plate model (`resources/kbdkid3-plate-left.FCStd`, one 19.05 cell per key, the right plate in it unused) aligned onto the keys by a pure translation (every cutout within 0.05 of the mean offset, one cutout per key) and put on the PCB, its outer edges trimmed 0.4, the switch-free corner between column 5, the thumb cell and the slant covered by a slab out to the slant plus 0.35 (north to column 5's top edge, east to the thumb cell's east edge), 4.17 head holes at the mounting drills, with `covered` S123's cell filled and a pit at its diode's place, then kbdkid4's `pattern_shift` (along +x) and `side_shave` (here also off the slanted edge, along its normal, leaving it 0.25 past the slant and 0.15 from the cavity wall), and the solids fused into one valid solid of unchanged volume. Every clip of the cell compound goes solid by solid, since a `common()` of the whole compound silently drops a cell on FreeCAD 1.0. `build_plate()` returns the left half's plate in the layout's frame, on the PCB (z 9.6 to 11.8), unshifted with `pattern_shift=0`. The module has no entry point, so other scripts import it. `scripts/export_plate.py` mirrors the plate for `right`, checks it against the layout and prints what it measured (inside the tray's cavity wall, taken as the outline moved out 0.4 with round corners, the slanted edge 0.25 out of the slant and the plate at least 0.15 from the cavity wall, both against DESIGN.md figures the script transcribes, not against the constants the plate is built from, every cutout on its key and every head hole on its drill, moved by the pattern shift), then writes it upside down (top face on z = 0, so the pit opens upward) as one closed mesh.

## Tray

`scripts/tray_shape.py` holds the tray's outline helpers and dimensions (ported from kbdkid4's) and `build_tray(right, pin_hole_diameter, fits)`, which builds a half's tray shell and joint from the layout, kbdkid4's tray pipeline on the layout instead of a STEP: walls and floor with every corner bent by the same concentric pair of radii, the ledge with the joint block in its place along the slant, the standoffs bored for M2 heat-set inserts, the dowel pin bosses and the parking hole's boss with the fills that close the crevices beside them, the ledge cut-aways `layout.py` declares for the parts in the tray, the sockets' notches, pockets and reliefs and the standoffs' flats exactly as `layout.py` gives them for the half, then the parts' mounts (`layout.mount_bodies()` fused one by one, then `layout.mount_voids()` cut: the USB shell's slip-fit channel through the wall's inner part and the USB window from the shell's mouth out, with kbdkid4's rounded corners and outer chamfer, the lever slot, the flat-topped teardrop reset pin hole, the solder grooves and the nice!nano's pry recess, then the nice!nano clamp's boss bored for its insert down to the floor's top), then the magnet slots (open toward the cavity) with the wall cut back over them, the flat-topped teardrop pin holes and parking hole, and the joint face's bottom chamfer. Each step checks its volume and probes what it made. Last it checks the floor closed (work item 009): the floor's underside must be the void-free footprint, the outside's bottom face less the joint face's chamfer, so no void reaches it. Every printed fit comes from `layout.printed_fit()`, the nominal size plus `PRINT_SWELLING_PER_SIDE_MM` on both sides plus the fit's clearance (a 45 degree face moves `PRINT_SWELLING_AT_45_DEGREES_MM` along an axis), and `pcb_shape.py`'s `pull_in` default is the same swelling. The right half is built in the left half's frame with its own socket cuts, pin hole fit, depth and lead-in, then mirrored by `mirror_for_right_half()`, so its pins and magnets sit at the left's places along the slant. `build_tray()` returns the tray unmirrored, so later work can add to it in the layout's frame. `build_clamp_tab()` builds the nice!nano clamp's tab, a print of its own, seated on the boss in the layout's frame. The module has no entry point. `scripts/export_tray.py` exports either half (`right`, and for a test print `pin_hole_diameter=<mm>` to override the half's pin hole fit, `nano_length`, `nano_width`, `clamp_press`, `bar_clearance` and `battery_shoulder` the mounts' `MountFits`. A figure that is an override is checked against it, one that follows from it only printed). It measures the tray against DESIGN.md's figures, transcribed in its own `DESIGN_*` constants (the outside, walls, floor, ledge and block, standoffs and bores, the joint face's angle, place and bottom chamfer, the pin holes with the plastic behind and over them, the magnet slots, the parking hole, every socket at least 0.5 from the tray, every mount's figures measured from DESIGN.md's board and walls, the clamp's tab (`build_clamp_tab()`) seated on its boss overlapping nothing of the tray, no plastic in the PCB connector's zone as printed or in `layout.py`'s wire rooms), then builds the other half and joins the two, each turned 17.74 degrees with the outer slant faces touching, and checks that they do not overlap, that the pin holes line up into one channel at least 1.0 longer than the pin and that the magnet slots face each other. It writes the tray floor on the bed as one closed mesh.

`scripts/export_clamp_tab.py` exports the nice!nano clamp's tab (work item 009), `tray_shape.py`'s `build_clamp_tab()`: one tab for both halves and every bay of the strip below, since its plan is symmetric about the board's center line and the boss's top sets the press. It measures the tab against its own `DESIGN_*` constants (the plate, the nose, the screw's hole) and writes it top face down, the nose standing up, as one closed mesh.

`scripts/export_nano_bay_test.py` exports the nice!nano bay test strip (work items 006 and 009): three bays, each `build_tray()`'s left tray with its own `MountFits` (`clamp_press` and `bar_clearance` stepping together, the defaults in bay 2, the press set by the boss's top under the one tab) cut by one box (`BAY_EAST_X_MM`, `BAY_SOUTH_Y_MM`), 2.0 apart, joined by floor bridges into one solid, bay k with k notches in its floor's south edge, written floor down on z = 0. `nano_length` and `nano_width` size the board for all three. It checks the box against every tray feature in plan (each wholly inside or outside, 0.5 from its faces, the nice!nano's mounts inside), its cutting faces against every vertex of the outer, cavity and ledge outlines (0.5 plus the corner's radius), and each bay's fits with `check_layout.py`'s mount problem functions (which take a `fits` for this), then one valid solid of the pieces' volume, the notches and a closed mesh.

## CI

`.github/workflows/ci.yml` has one job: it runs `check_layout.py`, builds the FreeCAD image, exports `kbdkid5_Pcb_Left.stl` and `kbdkid5_Pcb_Right.stl` (the word `right`) into `pcb/` and uploads them as the `pcb-stl` artifact, then exports the plate for both halves, plain and `covered`, into `plate/` (`kbdkid5_Plate_Left.stl`, `kbdkid5_Plate_Right.stl`, `kbdkid5_Plate_Left_Covered.stl`, `kbdkid5_Plate_Right_Covered.stl`) and uploads them as the `plate-stl` artifact, then exports the fused mock for both halves, the slit test strip plain and `wide` and the three socket test strips into `mock/` (`kbdkid5_Mock_Left.stl`, `kbdkid5_Mock_Right.stl`, `kbdkid5_Slit_Test.stl`, `kbdkid5_Slit_Test_Wide.stl`, `kbdkid5_Socket_Test_Pocket.stl`, `kbdkid5_Socket_Test_Lip.stl`, `kbdkid5_Socket_Test_Backing.stl`) and uploads them as the `mock-stl` artifact, then exports the tray for both halves, the nice!nano clamp's tab and the nice!nano bay test strip into `tray/` (`kbdkid5_Tray_Left.stl`, `kbdkid5_Tray_Right.stl`, `kbdkid5_Nano_Clamp_Tab.stl`, `kbdkid5_Nano_Bay_Test.stl`) and uploads them as the `tray-stl` artifact. Each artifact is uploaded as `kbdkid5-<that name>-<short commit hash>` (the head commit for a pull request, whose `github.sha` is a merge commit), so a download names the commit it was built from. The job is still called `pcb-mock`. A failing layout check fails the job before any export.

## Running the exports

Locally this machine has podman, not docker:

    podman build -t freecad-headless -f scripts/freecad.Dockerfile scripts
    podman run --rm -v "$PWD":/work -w /work freecad-headless freecadcmd scripts/<script>.py --pass <out.stl> [words]
    python3 scripts/check_layout.py

The freecadcmd quirks (exit codes swallowed, `__name__` set to the file stem, scripts run twice, dash arguments intercepted even after `--pass`, hence `key=value` words and `os._exit` through `run_and_exit`) are documented in the module docstring of `scripts/freecad_common.py`. Read it before writing a FreeCAD script here. The machine has 8 GB RAM: run at most two FreeCAD containers at once.

## Conventions

- Commit and push completed changes without asking. Keep commits small and focused.
- Test prints come first: the printer idling is the loop's bottleneck. Commit and push a test print (strip, mock, test piece) as soon as its export passes its own checks, since the user fetches STLs from CI's artifacts, then review and document while it prints, and put review findings into the next print. Scope a test print's work item to the printable: a shared change that pulls in the trays or plates is its own item. Verify with the export's checks and quick B-rep probes, never by turning STL meshes back into solids (one such probe ran over 13 minutes).
- `tmp/` is scratch space, `work/` holds untracked notes and downloads (the research's datasheets are in `work/research/downloads/`). Neither is committed.
- Test any change to an export script by running it in the container and checking the exit code; freecadcmd hides Python failures unless the script exits through `run_and_exit`.
- To inspect geometry beyond the exit code: `freecad_common.py`, `tray_shape.py`, `plate_shape.py`, `pcb_shape.py`, `socket_test_shape.py` and `layout.py` import normally (put `scripts/` on `sys.path`), but the export scripts must be exec'd with their trailing `run_and_exit(main)` line stripped and `__file__` predefined (a plain import would run main and `os._exit` the interpreter). Then drive their functions directly.
- Keep DESIGN.md, README.md, this file, the work items' status and the comments in the scripts current in the same commit as the change. Decisions go in a new dated file in `doc/log/` (write once, tagged with `#topic`).
- Markdown: one paragraph per line, never reflowed.
