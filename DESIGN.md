# kbdkid5 design

kbdkid5 is the successor of [kbdkid4](https://github.com/Tubbles/kbdkid4): a wireless split keyboard (Kailh Choc v1 on hotswap sockets, 19.05 mm pitch, a nice!nano v2 and a LiPo per half, ZMK), with a 3D-printed tray and switch plate. Its two changes:

1. **Nothing but the switch matrix on the PCB.** The PCB carries only the hotswap sockets, the diodes and one off-board connector. The nice!nano, the reset button, the power switch and the battery connector are wired with loose wires and mounted in the tray itself. The tray can then use the space under the PCB, and the half gets smaller.
2. **A slanted inner edge to join the halves.** The tray, PCB and plate are cut straight from the thumb key's top right corner to the rightmost top row key's top right corner. The two halves join along that slant with magnets and steel dowel pins into one Alice-like board (each half turned 17.7 degrees, rows dipping toward the middle), firm enough to type on in the lap.

The PCB itself is designed later (LibrePCB). Until then the geometry here is the ground truth, held in `scripts/layout.py`, and the PCB is a printed mock. Part choices and their sources are in [doc/components.md](doc/components.md).

## Frame and units

Millimetres. The left half in kbdkid4's board coordinates: x east, y north, the board's top side (keys) up, heights z above the tray floor's top face (the floor's underside is at z -1.2). The right half is the left half mirrored across the vertical line x = 66.199, the outline's center line, so a right part lies where its left one does. Two things differ: the joint's dowel holes (see below), and the switch footprints with their sockets, since a switch's two pins are not mirror symmetric. On the right half each key's footprint is the left half's, unmirrored, at the mirrored key center, as on kbdkid4's right half (its board turned over) seen from above, so its sockets reach toward the slant instead of away from it. This is a constraint on the PCB design: the right tray is cut for exactly these sockets (Tray) and the joint is placed to clear them (The joint), so a right half switch turned any other way, say 180 degrees, needs the right tray's cuts and the joint worked out again.

## Parts in the tray

Envelopes below are bodies only. `build_tray()` builds every part's mounts into both trays, each mount a box in `scripts/layout.py` (`MountBox`), the right half's in the left half's frame and mirrored with the tray (Mirroring, below). Every printed fit follows the joint's rule (The joint). A 45 degree face swells 0.283 along x, y or z (the 0.2 along its normal). Every mount body, and the nice!nano clamp's tab and screw head with them, keeps at least 0.5 from every part but its own (the tightest 1.8, the battery's northwest rib from the nice!nano), overlaps its own part nowhere (the closest 0.2 as modelled, a snug fit's swelling), stays out of the PCB connector's zone as printed (by 0.1, the nice!nano's north rib) and keeps 0.5 from the sockets: in plan where it reaches their height (only the clamp's screw head does, 1.686 from S101's socket on both halves) and in height where it comes under one, at least 1.08 below the nearest (the clamp's tab under S101). The mounts' voids keep at least 0.5 from the standoffs (the tightest 1.669, the east pin row's groove, from the standoff at (18.574, 37.782)), and the floor's voids stop short of its underside.

Wires need room behind every lead: 3.0 beyond each lead's tip, over the leads' row plus 1.0 to either side, from the floor up to 1.5 over the lead. Over the nice!nano's pads, whose wires are soldered on its top side, the room is 3.0 above the board, 0.5 beyond the pads. The battery's lead gets 3.0 along both edges at its corner. On both halves every room is clear of the tray as cut, the mounts, the standoffs, the joint, the sockets and the other parts, by at least 0.345 (the room over the nice!nano's west pin row, from the west ledge).

Printed floor down, the mounts need no support. Their only overhangs are bridges: the hold-down bar between its posts and on over the shell's channel to the mouth (9.44), the USB window's top (10.5 across, 6.6 of it flat between the corner rounds, as on kbdkid4's tray), the lever slot's top (4.0) and the reset pin hole's flat top (1.24). ### Mirroring

## The joint

The slanted tray wall's outer face, 1.2 out of the slant (the gap and the wall), is the joint face. Joined, the two halves' outer slant faces touch, each half turned 17.74 degrees. The pins and magnets sit at the same places along the slant on both halves, since the halves mate face to face. The joint face's bottom edge is chamfered 0.4 at 45 degrees along the whole slant, running out on the corner rounds, so elephant foot cannot keep the faces apart along the bed.

Every printed fit in the joint is modelled as the part's nominal size, plus the print's swelling of 0.2 per side (the mocks' `pull_in`) on both sides, plus the fit's clearance: 0.0 for a snug fit, 0.1 for a slip fit or a glued one. So it prints as the nominal size plus the clearance. The same swelling raises a bottom that faces up by 0.2 as printed, so wherever a part must fit over such a bottom (a notch's, a slot's, a recess's), the project counts its room from the bottom raised 0.2, and the plastic left under it where it is modelled, the thinner reading.

- **Block:** below the PCB, the slanted wall is 4.0 thick (normal to the face) from the floor up to the ledge top (z 8.0), replacing the ledge there. The PCB rests on it.
- **Magnets:** four supermagnete S-08-03-N (8 x 3, N45) per half, axis normal to the face, standing on edge, each glued against a 0.5 skin at the face, which takes its pull. Each slot is the magnet's glued fit, 8.5 along the slant and 3.5 across (8.1 and 3.1 printed), from z -0.7 up, 0.5 above the bed. The skin and the slot fill the block's 4.0, so the slot is a notch through the block, open at the top and toward the cavity, with no overhang to print. A wall on its inner side would need a thicker block, and the block cannot grow by more than 0.134 before the right half's S150 socket comes within 0.5 of it. Above the block the wall's inner face is still cut back to each slot up to the rim: the full wall would overhang the slot by 0.3, so a magnet could not go straight down into its place against the skin. The PCB covers the slots. They sit between the pins, at 14.24, 25.58, 36.92 and 48.26 along the slant from its south end, 2.99 from the pin bosses and 2.84 apart (spread evenly while the slots were 8.2 long, kept when they grew to 8.5). Five don't fit between the bosses, not even 1.0 from them and 2.2 apart (53.3 needed, 48.5 there). Glue them with alternating poles along the joint, the right half opposite to the left, so the halves pull together and center themselves. Two 0.5 skins leave a 1.0 gap between opposing magnets: about 6.0 N per pair after derating (doc/components.md), about 24 N in all. That only closes the joint. It cannot carry the bending of lap use.
- **Dowel pins:** two hardened ISO 8734 4 x 16 pins, normal to the face, at z 3.4 (midway between the bed and the PCB), at 3.5 and 59.0 along the slant from its south end (slant y 21.7 and 74.5), 55.5 apart, 8 embedded in each half. The pins are loose: to join, press them into the left half's holes, then push the right half on. They carry the bending and the shear: one keystroke at the joint loads the printed plastic around them with about 6.0 MPa by doc/components.md's formula and loads, against the 10 to 20 MPa assumed safe. That formula assumes a round bore, which bears on the pin over its whole diameter. The 003 review found that a full teardrop leaves the pin bearing upward on about a third of that width, roughly 20 MPa per keystroke. The holes' tops are therefore cut flat (below), meant to recover some of that bearing width. How much it recovers is not worked out. A hand resting on the joint would take 18 MPa on a round bore, but the user won't rest a hand there.
- **Dowel bosses:** each pin sits in a boss 7 across, from the floor up to the PCB's underside like the block (the PCB rests on it), reaching from the face past its hole's bottom: 9.4 into the tray on the left half, 10.0 on the right. The pins are as far apart as the joint face allows, against twist: each boss ends where the outline's slant ends (the upper one 0.006 short), which keeps it on the face's flat part between the tray's corner rounds (from 0.011 before the slant's south end to 0.051 past its north end). Their places along the slant do not enter the 6.0 MPa (each pin takes half the joint's bending), but 55.5 apart instead of 48.5 cuts the pins' load under a twist by an eighth. That also clears the right half's sockets (Frame and units): with the upper pin at 55.5, where it first sat, S150's socket overlapped its boss (then 8.2 deep) by 1.641. At 59.0 the bosses keep at least the ledge's 0.5 from every socket: S150's keeps 1.144 from the upper boss on the right half (3.701 on the left) and 0.634 from the block. Two fills, also from the floor to the PCB's underside, close the crevices that would open beside the bosses: one between the upper boss's north side, the north ledge's face and the block, out to the boss's inner end, and one north of the parking hole's boss up to the lower dowel boss (Pin parking).
- **Dowel holes:** teardrops, their point up since they print horizontal, cut flat at the circle's top, so the pin bears upward on a bridge (1.82 wide on the left, 1.86 on the right) instead of on the point's flanks, with 2.4 of plastic over them on the left half and 2.35 on the right. The left half's are the pin's snug fit, 4.4 across (4.0 printed), so a pin pressed in by hand stays put while the halves go together, and its stop: 8.2 deep (8.0 printed, the embedded length) with 1.2 of the boss behind. The right half's are a slip fit, 4.5 across (4.1 printed), 9.0 deep with 1.0 behind, their mouths chamfered 0.3 as a lead-in for the pin standing out of the left half. Joined, each pair makes a channel 17.2 long as modelled (16.8 printed) for the 16 pin. The slip fit leaves play: about 0.45 of opening at the joint's bottom before the pins bear (the 003 review's estimate), until a fit test tightens it. Neither fit has been printed yet.
- **Voids:** any two of the joint's holes and slots and the parking hole keep at least 1.0 of plastic between them. The tightest are the lower dowel hole and the parking hole, 2.09 apart on the left half and 1.80 on the right.
- **Pin parking:** in split mode each half carries one pin, in a parking hole in the thumb key's short east wall below the slant: a teardrop hole flat-topped like the dowel holes, the pin's slip fit (4.5 across, 4.1 printed), along -x at y 13.1 (the middle of the thumb cell's socket-free north part, y 7.84 to 18.3) and z 3.4, in a boss under the PCB. The pin goes in 14.5, so its end stands 1.5 proud of the wall to grip with the fingers, and a supermagnete S-04-02-N (4 x 2, N45, about 4.1 N to steel) glued at the hole's bottom holds it: the hole is 16.5 deep, its last 2.0 the magnet's seat, whose glued fit comes to the hole's own 4.5. Its boss is 7 across like the dowel bosses (y 9.6 to 16.6), reaches 1.5 behind the hole's bottom and stands from the floor to the PCB's underside, which rests on it, with 2.35 of plastic over the hole, 1.763 clear of the thumb's socket on both halves. A fill extends the boss north to the lower dowel boss, which it merges with: the triangle between the boss's north side, the outline's east edge and the dowel boss's south side. The hole stays clear of the thumb's socket (by 3.013) and the standoff at (114.141, 19.765) (by 2.414). Hardened plain steel pins are magnetic. Stainless ones are not, so don't buy those.
